<!--
  DELIBERATELY NOT A TRANSFORMING BOX. Nothing survives a cut here. Each beat is its own world, its own
  palette, its own prop, and the film holds because no clause ever finishes: every beat ends mid-sentence
  and the next one picks it up. That is the reference grammar, studied in refs/pin-522769469268499616.mp4.
  The gate cannot see a sentence, so this carries a waiver naming the thread.

  The palette FLIPS beat to beat (dark, light, dark, light, dark). A held backdrop would be the safe call
  and would waste the freedom the sentence thread pays for.
-->

## Beat 1: You built it by hand (0s-2.8s)
- type: hook
- object: none; the clause arrives alone in a dark field
- shot: medium
- camera: hold
- picture: a hand-built timeline drawn as stacked bars, lit from one side, most of the frame empty and dark
- mechanism: per-word reveal with ONE word carrying the colour, the rest muted
- becomes: an empty dark field becomes a stack of hand-placed bars
- onscreen: You built it <b>by hand</b>,
- narration: You built it by hand,
- why: open mid-thought so the first cut is a comma and not a door
- emotion: recognition
- duration: 2.8s
- transition_in: cut

## Beat 2: and it looked good (2.8s-5.4s)
- type: build
- object: none; new world, light
- shot: wide
- camera: slowPush
- picture: the finished thing on a bright workbench, warm and settled, nothing wrong with it
- mechanism: palette FLIP to light on the cut · slow push · the clause typing on
- becomes: the dark workshop becomes a bright bench where the work looks finished
- onscreen: and it <b>looked good</b>,
- narration: and it looked good,
- why: the setup has to be genuinely attractive or the reversal is not a reversal
- emotion: satisfaction
- duration: 2.6s
- transition_in: cut

## Beat 3: until the number moved (5.4s-8.2s)
- type: problem
- object: none; the world goes red
- shot: medium
- camera: hold
- picture: one figure ticking over and every bar around it going out of alignment, lit red
- mechanism: count roll on the figure · the bars shifting out of true beneath it · palette flip to red-dark
- becomes: a settled bench becomes a wall of numbers that no longer agree
- onscreen: until the <b>number moved</b>,
- narration: until one number moved,
- why: the turn, and it lands on a picture of the damage rather than a word for it
- emotion: sinking
- duration: 2.8s
- transition_in: cut

## Beat 4: and now it is Friday again (8.2s-11.2s)
- type: agitation
- object: none; a calendar wall
- shot: wide
- camera: hold
- picture: a wall of identical days with the same task written on every one of them
- mechanism: the days filling in one after another, faster than the eye tracks, then stopping
- becomes: one changed number becomes every week of the rest of the year
- onscreen: and now it is <b>Friday</b> again.
- narration: and now it is Friday again.
- why: the cost is repetition, so the frame has to repeat until it is uncomfortable
- emotion: dread
- duration: 3.0s
- transition_in: cut

## Beat 5: describe it once (11.2s-15.0s)
- type: payoff
- object: none; the file, alone
- shot: medium
- camera: hold
- picture: a single file, small and still, with the whole wall of days collapsing into it
- mechanism: the calendar wall converging inward · the file holding still while everything else leaves
- becomes: a year of Fridays becomes one file you describe once
- onscreen: <b>Describe it once.</b>
- narration: So describe it once instead.
- why: the sentence finally reaches a full stop, on the thing that ends the loop
- emotion: relief
- duration: 3.8s
- transition_in: cut
