This video tells people who have only ever seen frosted glass that a blur softens what is behind
it, and this bends it and splits it into three.

The ground is white and stays white, which is the hard version of this brief: refraction needs
detail behind it, and a busy ground is the cheap way to supply it. Here the detail is the subject.

## Beat 1: The word, and a lens with nothing to bend (0s-3.9s)
- type: hook
- object: the lens, entering from off-frame left at 0.9 scale over empty paper
- shot: wide. The word fills the upper third, the lower half is deliberately empty
- camera: hold
- picture: REFRACTION set at 196px in ink on warm paper, and a glass sphere crossing the left third where there is nothing behind it but the ground
- onscreen: "REFRACTION" / "a blur softens."
- mechanism: hand-keyed motion track, linear interior, seven keys · a drifting radial on the backdrop
- becomes: an empty circle of glass becomes a lens, the moment it reaches the first letter and the letter tears
- trigger: nothing yet. The film opens on its subject rather than assembling one, because the first frame of a clip is its poster frame
- layout: word in the middle third, lower half empty
- style: warm paper, one weight of ink, no accent colour anywhere
- rest: the ground drifts 1.15% per second under everything. Nothing else idles
- why: state the Before in three words and let the lens walk into the claim
- emotion: recognition
- duration: 3.9s
- transition_in: cut

## Beat 2: It reaches the type (3.9s-8.2s)
- type: proof
- object: the lens mid-word, the letterforms inside it compressed and split red to blue at the rim
- shot: medium. The lens owns the right half, the slab takes the lower fifth and then the lower third
- camera: hold
- picture: the same word, now with a moving hole in it, plus an ink slab rising from the bottom edge
- onscreen: "glass bends."
- mechanism: the lens track continues at a near-constant scale · the slab arrives on its own hand-keyed track, rests as a band, then surges
- becomes: a flat word becomes a word with a lens through it, and an empty lower frame becomes a hard ink horizon
- trigger: the lens ran out of empty ground in beat 1 and arrived at the only thing on screen
- layout: type across the middle third, ink filling the lower third by the end of the beat
- style: the ground turns from warm paper to cool. The only colour in the film is the dispersion fringe
- rest: none on the spectacle. The slab holds dead still for 1.6s before it moves, so the surge has something to break
- why: this is the whole claim, shown rather than described. Type is the densest detail on a white frame
- emotion: surprise
- duration: 4.3s
- transition_in: blur

## Beat 3: One straight edge, bent (8.2s-10.9s)
- type: payoff
- object: the lens settled on the horizon, the bend held still to be read
- shot: medium-wide. Lens right of centre on the horizon, type left, ink across the lower third
- camera: hold
- picture: the ink horizon running straight across the frame and bowing where it passes through the lens's outer third
- onscreen: "Refraction, in CSS." / "one displacement map."
- mechanism: the lens decelerates on easyEase handles and drifts 24px right through the end card
- becomes: a demonstration becomes a claim you can check, because the bent edge is still on screen while the line naming the recipe is read
- trigger: the surge in beat 2 put the horizon inside the lens, and the film has to stop moving so the result can be looked at
- layout: title and line in the upper left, lens on the horizon at the right, the two never overlapping
- style: the coolest of the three grounds, ink slab, one line of mono
- rest: the lens drifts. The bend does not change
- why: name the recipe while the proof is still in frame, so nobody has to take it on trust
- emotion: conviction
- duration: 2.7s
- transition_in: blur
