Vawe has a lot of gates and they are strict. They will tell you a caption is under the platform chrome,
that a transition flashes, that a beat holds nothing. **Not one of them can tell you the film is worth
watching.** They read structure. Structure is necessary and it is not the thing.

So the engine ships six ways to put frames in front of you, and they are not interchangeable. Each one
hides what the next one shows.

<Callout type="warn">
**Producing an image is not looking at one.** `make ship` generates the sheets whether you open them or
not, and `beat-check` will tell you nobody has read the current version. That check exists because
"the file is on disk" stopped being evidence that a person had looked.
</Callout>

## The six, and what each one is blind to

| Tool | Shows | Blind to |
|---|---|---|
| `make beats` | first, middle and last frame of every beat | how anything MOVES |
| `make reveal` | the enter arc, the settled frame, the exit arc | whether the beat was worth having |
| `make studio` | a live scrubber and a timeline of every layer | anything you do not scrub to |
| `make seam-check` | the frames straddling every transition | the middle of a beat |
| `make judge` | key frames plus a scoring rubric | motion, entirely |
| `make scrub` | the whole film as one contact sheet | detail, at that size |

### `make beats` shows the holds

```bash
make beats D=formats/scene/<film>.json      # → /tmp/beats/<film>.png
```

Three frames per beat. This is the fastest way to catch murk, an overlap, a beat that lands on nothing.
It samples the MIDDLE of each beat, which is exactly why it cannot show you an entrance: by the time it
samples, everything has arrived.

### `make reveal` shows the arrival

```bash
make reveal D=formats/scene/<film>.json     # → /tmp/reveal/<film>.png
```

Per beat: the enter arc, the settled frame, the exit arc, pulled from the real layer start times. This
is where you see the dolly direction, the typing, the colour wave, the stagger. **Judging a film on
settled frames is how a recreation loses the one thing that made the original good**, and this page
exists because that happened here.

### `make studio` is the only one that moves

```bash
make studio D=formats/scene/<film>.json
```

A frame scrubber (drag, step, space to play) over a timeline: one bar per layer against a seconds
ruler, cuts and seams and stings marked, enter and exit ramps shaded off the settled middle, and every
dead-air hole painted as a hazard band. **The contact sheets sample. This is where you see structure.**

### `make seam-check` is the cheapest catch for the worst bugs

```bash
make seam-check D=formats/scene/<film>.json  # → /tmp/seams/<film>.png
```

It pulls the frames straddling every transition out of the rendered mp4 and flags a luminance flash in
the overlap. A one-frame black flash is invisible in every center-sampling tool and horrible to watch.
This finds it in seconds.

### `make judge` is the scored one

```bash
make judge D=formats/scene/<film>.json      # → /tmp/judge/sheet.png + a rubric
```

Score every frame 1 to 5 on **readability, hierarchy, composition, brand fidelity, asset fidelity,
produced-not-generated, and value.** The last one is the hardest to be honest about on your own work,
which is why it is worth scoring separately rather than as a general impression.

## What to actually look for

A checklist beats a vibe, because a vibe finds whatever you were already worried about.

1. **Anything unreadable.** Contrast, clipping, a caption under the platform chrome.
2. **A seam that flashes.** Worst defect per unit of effort to find.
3. **A beat with no focal point.** If your eye does not know where to land, neither does anyone's.
4. **A beat that is only type.** Ask what it could SHOW: a bar whose length IS the number, the real
   product surface, a diagram.
5. **A dead backdrop.** Most films in this library paint one backdrop for the entire runtime.
6. **Passive whitespace.** Name what the empty part of the frame is doing in one clause. If enlarging
   the subject removes it and improves the frame, it was never working.

## The rule that makes this worth doing

**If your eye catches a flaw, it is a fix, never a rationalisation.** The temptation after a green gate
run is to decide the thing you noticed is fine. It is not fine; the gates simply cannot see it. That is
the entire reason this page exists.

And the converse, which matters just as much: **when satisfying a gate requires making the film worse,
suspect the gate.** A gate that measures the wrong thing does not merely miss defects, it manufactures
them, and you pay by deforming something you can see is right until a number moves.
