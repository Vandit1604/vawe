The checks tell you a caption is clipped, a cue is late or a hold is too short. No check can tell you the film is worth watching. Structure is necessary. It is not the goal.

<Callout type="warn">
Producing an image is not looking at one. Open the sheet and look at it.
</Callout>

## The tools

| Command | Shows |
|---|---|
| `bin/vawe compare --page <page> --at 2.5,3.1,4` | your frames at exact seconds, about 4 s for 4 frames, no render needed. Add `--ref <mp4>` for the reference beside it |
| `bin/vawe dev <page>` | a half-size draft, plus a key-frame sheet PNG next to it |
| `bin/vawe critique <page>` | a phone sheet, a strip, the loop seam, measured deltas and the `page-check` findings |
| `bin/vawe review <page>` | one report and one sheet: clipped glyphs, text timing, motion, coverage, worst first |
| `bin/vawe studio <page>` | a live scrubber. It is the only tool that moves |
| `bin/vawe judge <page or mp4>` | key frames and a rubric for a fresh session to score |

Never render a whole range to look at a few frames. Use `compare --page` for frames and `dev --from --to` for motion.

## Critique in a fresh session

Give the draft to a session that did not write the page (`vawe-critique`). It rejects by default. It names the shot, the frame and the fix. Fix the top items, re-render only those seconds, and critique again.

`bin/vawe judge <page or mp4> --runs A,B` prepares two runs for two fresh sessions. `bin/vawe judge --fresh <page> --brief <brief.md>` scores now with a separate headless session and advises, never blocks. A pass is never recorded by the author of the page.

## Match a reference

```bash
bin/vawe spec refs/ad.mp4                              # SPEC.md and spec.json
bin/vawe critique <page> --ref refs/ad.mp4
bin/vawe coverage out/film.mp4 --ref refs/ad.mp4       # SSIM at every step
```

`coverage` exits 1 when any second falls below `--min` (default 0.5). `bin/vawe ship` refuses a recreation until the `critique --ref` loop passes.

## What to look for

1. Anything unreadable: contrast, clipping, a line that leaves too fast.
2. A beat with no focal point. If your eye does not know where to land, neither does anyone's.
3. A beat that is only type. Ask what it could show instead.
4. A frame that is static at the tail. Turn the world every one or two beats.
5. Two things fighting for attention.

## The rule that makes this worth doing

If your eye catches a flaw, it is a fix, not a rationalisation. A green check run does not mean the thing you noticed is fine. The checks cannot see it.

When a check asks you to make the film worse, suspect the check.
