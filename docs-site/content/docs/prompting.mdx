Most people never hand-write a scene file. They describe the film and let an AI agent compose the JSON,
then iterate on the render. Vawe is built for that: `CLAUDE.md` at the repo root is a long standing
brief the agent reads before it writes anything, so the agent already knows the house rules, the
vocabulary and the quality gates.

Your job is much smaller than you think.

## The five lines

This is the whole prompt. Any of them may be missing, and the agent will ask if a missing one would
change the film.

```
SUBJECT   what it is about, in one line
DATA      where the facts come from (a URL, a file, an API, or "here they are")
PAYOFF    the one thing to remember, and it lands LAST
AUDIENCE  who watches, and where they see it
FEELING   one reference, or one word ("cinematic", "loud", "like Linear's launch page")
```

<Callout type="warn">
**If you find yourself naming a colour, an easing, a layer type or a preset, stop.** That is the
agent's job, not yours. Reaching for engine vocabulary is a sign the brief above is underspecified,
and the fix is a better PAYOFF or FEELING line, not a hex code.
</Callout>

## What you are NOT deciding

Palette, typography, layout, motion, camera, cuts, backdrop, sound, pacing, structure. All of it
belongs to whoever composes the scene, and all of it is defensible from the brief. A film that gets
these wrong got a thin brief, not a wrong colour.

Two lines are the agent's to fill in, not yours, and they are written into the plan before authoring:

| Line | What it does |
|---|---|
| `SPECTACLE` | the ONE exaggerated moment, named: which beat, which layer, which device |
| `NOT` | what this film explicitly does not do (no narration, no stock photos, no gradient hero) |

`SPECTACLE` is two-sided on purpose. Naming the loud moment is also a promise that every other beat
stays restrained. A film where four beats shout has no loud moment, it has a volume setting.

`NOT` is cheaper than it looks. Most generic output is not a wrong decision, it is an un-excluded
default: nothing said "no gradient hero", so a gradient hero was free.

## Two shapes of prompt

**Cold start.** You have an idea and nothing else.

```
SUBJECT   why deterministic rendering matters for CI
PAYOFF    the same input always produces the same bytes
AUDIENCE  developers, on a landing page, muted autoplay
FEELING   dry and technical, like a good changelog
```

**Warm start.** You have a source: a website, a dataset, a script, a competitor's film.

```
SUBJECT   our launch, reflecting the real product UI
DATA      https://example.com  (crawl it, do not just read the homepage)
PAYOFF    it ships in one click
AUDIENCE  X and LinkedIn, 16:9 and 9:16
FEELING   like the site itself, no louder
```

A warm start is usually better, because a real source carries taste the agent does not have to invent.
When you point at a website, say **crawl it**, not "look at it": the interesting surface is rarely the
homepage.

## The loop

Once the brief is agreed, the work is a loop, not a single shot.

```bash
make dev  D=formats/scene/<film>.json    # build, draft render, open. No gates, no waiting.
make check D=formats/scene/<film>.json   # every gate, every finding, ZERO consequence
make ship  D=formats/scene/<film>.json   # the ladder with its teeth in
```

`make dev` is where nearly all the time goes. `make ship` is the one command that says a film is done:
it runs the author checks, renders, audits the layout and inspects the transitions, in that order.

<Callout>
**Green gates do not mean the film is good.** They read structure. They cannot see murk, a weak focal
point, or a beat that lands wrong. Read the contact sheets: `make beats` for what each beat holds,
`make reveal` for how each one enters and exits, `make judge` for a scored sheet and a rubric.
</Callout>

## What to say when it is wrong

Be concrete about the SYMPTOM and leave the cause alone. "Beat three reads as a slide, nothing moves
but the text" is worth ten times "make it more dynamic". The agent can find the cause; it cannot guess
what you saw.

If you want a different direction rather than a fix, go back to `FEELING` and `NOT`. Those two lines
move a film further than any note about a specific layer.
