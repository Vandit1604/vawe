# One object survives a cut and changes across it, or name what else holds the film

A slideshow is beats born and dying inside their own window: every cut jumps between unrelated shots.
The cheapest fix is a continuous object: one element that crosses the cut and changes on the far
side, or a match cut where a shape in shot A becomes a shape in shot B. Murch ranks continuity of
this kind last of six things a cut must serve, so name three ways the film could hold its subject
and reject the first before defaulting to it.

A real alternative also holds a film: a sound bridge (a voiceover sentence that spans the cut), a
recurring motif, or an escalation. Use one on purpose. Do not fake a continuous object to tick the
rule.
