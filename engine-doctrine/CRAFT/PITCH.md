# PITCH: diverge before the storyboard converges

A request like "make a video about the launch" has no field to ask about yet. Asking for the message
hands the user back the blank they came to fill. Run one pitch round first, then converge.

Why: an early Hinge cut was a serif headline, two couple photos and a tagline. It rendered clean and
was still the median: the film anyone asked for "a Hinge ad" makes first. No check catches that. Only a
second question does: is this the film a hundred other attempts would land on?

## Four questions (ask yourself, do not show them)

1. What does the subject look like in its own visual world? Name the objects. A tax product is paper,
   stamps and dates. A running shoe is impact and stride.
2. What does the target emotion look like as a frame? Longing is empty space. Urgency is compression.
   Awe is one element too large for its container.
3. What does the playback surface demand? A feed fights for the first second. A lobby screen has minutes.
4. What does every other video on this subject look like? That is the anti-pattern.

## Five concepts, one per path

1. The subject's own world (a captured surface, a real number counting up).
2. The emotion staged as a frame.
3. The audience, met or deliberately broken.
4. The anti-pattern, inverted (question 4, structural opposite).
5. An unusual format: a letter, a countdown, a recipe, a front page, a map.

## Gates

- Estimate for each concept the chance that a model given this brief with no guidance would produce it
  first. At least two must sit below 0.10, or the set is not diverse: resample, push on paths 4 and 5.
  This is a constraint on your own generation, never a claim of measured fact.
- Sketch each concept as rough boxes (hero mass, type, how full the frame is). Two concepts with one
  silhouette are one concept. Replace one.

## Presenting

Three lines per concept: the concept, the visual world, the literal first thing on screen. Show all
five, then recommend one with a one-sentence reason. Mixing two is a valid answer. Silence accepts the
recommendation. This is one round, not an iteration loop. The chosen concept is the brief's `angle`.

Autonomous ("just build it"): run the same round, pick yourself, and name in one line the most typical
direction you left behind. Requester without video vocabulary: skip the pitches and give a short
decision map (where it plays, how long, what register), each with a marked default.
